# Testing the Battery key

- [When to run these checks](#when-to-run-these-checks)
- [Setup](#setup)
- [Checks](#checks)

The unit tests check against emulated receivers how the plugin reads the battery, through which feature and with which request, and what it makes of each level and charging state. They also check against a fake Stream Deck which image each reading draws on the keys, on the background and with the mouse, the battery and the bolt each key's settings choose, which color each percentage takes on the levels each key chooses, that a press and the 10-minute timer read the battery again, that the timer stops once no key is shown, and that a mouse that doesn't answer greys the last reading out, and against the settings page's HTML that it shows the plugin's default background, mouse, battery, bolt and levels, and heads each section and ends it with a Reset button, and that the controls its script greys out are on the page. The checks below cover what they take for granted: that the mouse answers as the probe measured it, that Stream Deck draws the image the plugin sends, at every key size, that the key follows the mouse as it charges, leaves and comes back, and that the settings page, which no test loads, saves what it shows.

## When to run these checks

- After a change to `src/battery.ts`, `src/mouse.ts`, `src/actions/battery.ts` or `src/actions/battery-image.ts`, to the key's images or to its settings page.
- After an update of `@daczarne/streamdeck-icons`, whose glyphs, palette and figures the key is drawn from.
- After an upgrade of `node-hid`, `@elgato/streamdeck` or `@elgato/utils`.
- After a Stream Deck or macOS update.

## Setup

The mouse is paired with a Logi Bolt receiver on each host it switches between (see [the probes' README][probes]), and is on the channel of the computer the Deck is on, whose receiver is plugged in. Place a Battery key on the Deck, and another on a Deck with smaller keys, if there's one. Build the branch, quit Stream Deck and open it again when the branch changes `manifest.json`, and otherwise restart the plugin:

```shell
npm run build
npx streamdeck restart com.daczarne.mx3s
```

To read the end of the log:

```shell
tail -n 5 com.daczarne.mx3s.sdPlugin/logs/mx3s.log
```

## Checks

1. **The key shows the battery.** As soon as it's placed, the key shows the mouse in white, the battery filled about as far as the percentage, and the percentage under them, centered, with no title. The percentage is the one in the log's last `Read the MX Master 3S's battery` line, and the battery and the percentage are green from 40%, yellow from 15%, and red below that, whatever level the line says.
2. **It reads on every key size.** On the smallest key placed, the percentage reads at a glance, and the mouse, the battery and the bolt stay apart. A percentage of 100, when the mouse is full, fits the key.
3. **A press reads the battery again.** Press the key: the log ends with `Key pressed`, then a new `Read the MX Master 3S's battery` line, and the key shows that reading.
4. **The bolt follows the cable.** Plug the mouse's USB-C cable in, and press the key: a bolt shows on its right, green on a key as placed, and the log's last line says `charging`. Unplug the cable, and press the key again: the bolt is gone, and the line says `not charging`.
5. **The key greys out when the mouse is elsewhere.** Send the mouse to another host with its own button, then press the key: it shows the mouse and the last reading's percentage in grey, without the battery and the bolt, and the log ends with `No receiver reaches the MX Master 3S`. Bring the mouse back, and press the key: its colors come back.
6. **The key shows dashes before the first reading.** Unplug this computer's receiver, and restart the plugin: the key shows the mouse in grey, with three dashes, and the log says `No Logi Bolt receiver found`. Plug the receiver back in, and press the key: it shows the reading.
7. **The key reads again every 10 minutes.** Leave the key shown, without pressing it, for a little over 10 minutes: the log has a new `Read the MX Master 3S's battery` line about 10 minutes after the one before.
8. **Every key shows the same reading, and none reads while none is shown.** With two Battery keys shown, press one: both show the new reading, and the log has one `Read` line. Switch to a profile or page with no Battery key for a little over 10 minutes: the log has no `Read` line in that time.
9. **Each key keeps the background it chooses.** On a key's settings page, the background shows the charcoal of the key as placed, with **None** beside it on the same row. Pick another color: the key takes it once the picker closes, and the log has no new `Read` line. Check **None**: the key turns black behind the mouse, the battery and the percentage, whatever the color picked, and back to that color once unchecked. Another Battery key keeps its own background throughout, and both keep theirs through the next press and across a restart of the plugin.
10. **Each key keeps the mouse it chooses.** On a key's settings page, **Mouse icon** shows **Show** checked, with **Filled** chosen beside it, and under them the white of the key as placed, with **Follow battery level** unchecked beside it. Choose **Outlined**: the mouse turns to its outline. Pick another color: the mouse takes it once the picker closes, and the log has no new `Read` line. Check **Follow battery level**: the mouse takes the color of the percentage, and the color picker greys out. Uncheck it: the mouse takes the color picked again. Uncheck **Show**: the mouse goes, and the battery, the bolt and the percentage stay where they were. Send the mouse to another host and press the key: a mouse given a color, or following the level, turns grey with the rest of the key, and a mouse not shown comes back, in grey. Another Battery key keeps its own mouse throughout, and both keep theirs across a restart of the plugin.
11. **Each key keeps the battery it chooses.** On a key's settings page, **Battery icon** shows **Show** checked, and under it a green color picker, greyed out, with **Follow battery level** checked beside it. Uncheck **Follow battery level**: the color picker can be used, and the battery turns green, whatever its level. Pick another color: the battery takes it once the picker closes, the percentage keeps its level's color, and the log has no new `Read` line. Check **Follow battery level** again: the battery takes its level's color, and the picker greys out. Uncheck **Show**: the battery goes, and the mouse, the bolt and the percentage stay where they were. Check it again: the battery comes back, in the color it had. Another Battery key keeps its own battery throughout, and both keep theirs across a restart of the plugin.
12. **Each key keeps the bolt it chooses.** On a key's settings page, **Bolt icon** shows **Charging** chosen, between **Always** and **Never**, on one line, and under it, **Charging** shows the green of the key's bolt, with **Follow battery level** unchecked beside it, and **Not charging** a dim slate, greyed out until **Always** is chosen. With the cable unplugged, choose **Always**: a slate bolt shows on the right. Plug the cable in, and press the key: the bolt turns green. Pick another color under **Charging**: the bolt takes it once the picker closes, without a new `Read` line. Check **Follow battery level**: the bolt takes the color of the percentage, and the **Charging** color greys out. Unplug the cable, press the key, and the bolt takes the **Not charging** color. Uncheck **Follow battery level**, plug the cable in and press the key: the bolt takes the color picked again. Unplug the cable, press the key, and pick another color under **Not charging**: the bolt takes it. Choose **Never**: the bolt goes, whether the cable is plugged in or not, and the mouse, the battery and the percentage stay where they were. Another Battery key keeps its own bolt throughout, and both keep theirs across a restart of the plugin.
13. **Each section resets its own settings.** On a key's settings page, the sections are headed **Background**, **Mouse icon**, **Battery icon**, **Bolt icon**, **Battery level** and **Mouse not found**, each with a line under its heading and a **Reset** button at its end, and only **Charging** and **Not charging**, **High**, **Medium** and **Low**, and **Show** and **Color**, have labels on the left. In each section, change every setting that isn't greyed out, such as a color picked by hand, then press its **Reset**: the section's controls show their defaults again, the picker's color included, the key redraws as placed for that section, and the other sections keep what they were given. Reopen the page by selecting another key and this one again: it still shows the defaults.
14. **Settings that change nothing grey out.** On a key's settings page, check **None** under **Background**: the color picker greys out, and comes back once it's unchecked. Uncheck **Show** under **Mouse icon**: **Filled**, **Outlined** and the color picker grey out. Under **Bolt icon**, with **Charging** chosen, **Not charging** is greyed out. Choose **Always**: both colors can be picked. Choose **Never**: both grey out. With a section's choice off, press its **Reset**: its settings come back, no longer greyed out. Uncheck **Show** under **Mouse icon** or **Battery icon**, or choose **Never** under **Bolt icon**: that icon's **Follow battery level** greys out with its colors. With an icon shown, check its **Follow battery level**: its color picker greys out, and comes back once it's unchecked, while the bolt's **Not charging** follows **Always** alone. Leave the mouse hidden and the bolt on **Never**, select another key and this one again: the page opens with them greyed out.
15. **Each key keeps the levels it chooses.** With the mouse under 100%, on a key's settings page, **Battery level** shows **High** green **from 40%**, **Medium** yellow from **15%** to **40%**, on one row, and **Low** red **below 15%**, with the dropdowns going from 100% at the top to 0% at the bottom. Set **Medium**'s second percentage to **100%**: the battery and the percentage turn yellow, without a new `Read` line, and **High** says **from 100%**. Set its first percentage to **100%** too: they turn red, and **Low** says **below 100%**. Pick another color under **Low**: they take it once the picker closes. Set the first percentage to **0%**, with the second still at **100%**: they turn yellow, and **Low** says **below 0%**. Swap them, the first at **100%** and the second at **0%**: the key stays yellow, and **High** and **Low** still say **from 100%** and **below 0%**. Press **Reset**: the levels come back as placed. Another Battery key keeps its own levels throughout, and both keep theirs across a restart of the plugin.
16. **Each key keeps what it shows once the mouse isn't found.** On a key's settings page, **Mouse not found** shows **Show** with **Mouse** checked and **Battery** and **Bolt** unchecked, on one row, and **Color** the grey of the key as placed. Send the mouse to another host with its own button, and press the key: it shows the mouse and the last reading's percentage in grey, and nothing else. Check **Battery**: the battery shows, filled as far as the last reading, in grey, without a new `Read` line. Check **Bolt**: the bolt shows, in grey, whatever **Bolt icon** is set to. Uncheck **Mouse**: the mouse goes, and the battery, the bolt and the percentage stay where they were. Pick another color: everything the key shows takes it once the picker closes. Choose **Outlined** under **Mouse icon**, and check **Mouse** again: the mouse shows, outlined. Uncheck **Show** under **Mouse icon** and **Battery icon**, and choose **Never** under **Bolt icon**: the mouse, the battery and the bolt still show. Uncheck **Mouse**, **Battery** and **Bolt** here, and choose **Always** under **Bolt icon**: only the percentage shows. Check **Mouse** again. Bring the mouse back, and press the key: it shows what its other sections choose, in their colors. Press this section's **Reset**: it comes back as placed. Another Battery key keeps its own choices throughout, and both keep theirs across a restart of the plugin.
